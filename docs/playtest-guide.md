# First proper Boat Race playtest

Use the GitHub Pages website as the primary preview, or a locally hosted production build. The portable HTML remains available for standalone use. For hosted offline play, wait for **Offline ready** before disconnecting. Keep the console closed during the child-facing session; the game should explain itself.

## A short shared session

1. Let a child choose a challenge and reject/accept boats without coaching. Can they explain the gamble after seeing four offers?
2. Try a normal 20-second scramble. Notice whether category shortcuts, item names and three equipment slots are understood. Repeat untimed if reading/touch time feels tight.
3. Play Fastest with a propulsion part, Slowest with drag/braking equipment, Picnic with different glide, and Wobbly with stability/flotation. Ask what changed and why they think it changed.
4. In Picnic, watch whether the target appears soon enough and the stopping estimate supports a deliberate brake. In Wobbly, watch whether warning timing makes brace feel learnable.
5. Try a silly combination (balloons + rock, spoon + fizz, fan + sail). Does the event/result feedback make the consequence understandable?
6. Let someone need a rescue. Can they identify what happened, find themselves in the results and want another go?
7. Follow Lantern’s gold waterline marker and YOU label. Are all four racers identifiable? Try new seeds: all five rivals should appear across sessions, with two cheering at results. Compare raft, welly and basket poses, including three overhead/water equipment items.
8. On the actual tablet/smartboard, try touch, keyboard focus, reduced motion, pause, leaving/resuming and offline replay. Check boat/character visibility from the back of the room.

## Capture useful feedback

Record approximate age, device/browser, challenge, boat, equipment, where instructions were missed, and what made them laugh. Use **Save race log** on results to capture seed, fleet setup, accepted action ticks, events and results. The log is local JSON, contains no account data and is not uploaded automatically. Source simulation is deterministic for the same seed, loadout and action timing; logs document actions/events/results, but there is no built-in replay player.

Balance is deliberately asymmetric. Look for repeated unbeatable choices, useful parts that nobody notices, mandatory-feeling combinations, too much waiting after Lantern finishes, cramped gear displays, warning overload and unreadable controls. Review several seeds rather than a single surprising loss. Cosmetic debris is not automatically damage.

## Suggested iteration order

Prioritise reading/decision time and Picnic braking clarity, then seven-boat seating/mount alignment at race size, then Wobbly difficulty and equipment usefulness. Add richer sound or more encounter variations after children understand and enjoy the core loop. Test Safari/iPad and Firefox before broad classroom distribution.
